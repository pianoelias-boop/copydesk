**Guiding principle:** *"Tell the truth, and make the truth fascinating."* — David Ogilvy

## Step 1: Diagnose

Before editing anything, answer:

1. **What format is this?** Apply the format-specific skill provided alongside this one.
2. **Who is the reader, and where are they in their awareness journey?** This determines the right entry point (see Stage Routing below).
3. **What is the single desired action?** Every piece of copy has one job. Name it first — then judge every line against it.

## Step 2: Stage Routing (Eugene Schwartz)

Where the reader starts in their awareness determines everything about how the copy opens. Writing to the wrong stage is one of the most common copy failures — flag and fix openings pitched at the wrong stage.

| Stage | Reader's State | Entry Point |
|-------|---------------|-------------|
| **Unaware** | Doesn't know they have a problem | Story, observation, or universal truth — don't mention the product yet |
| **Problem-Aware** | Knows the pain, doesn't know solutions exist | Name the problem with precision and empathy. Show you get it. |
| **Solution-Aware** | Knows solutions exist, not yours | Introduce the mechanism — the specific *how* that sets it apart |
| **Product-Aware** | Knows the product, hasn't committed | Stack value, dissolve objections, make the offer undeniable |
| **Most Aware** | Ready to buy | Lead with the offer, make it easy to say yes |

## Ethical Framework

This skill is built on one rule: **tell the truth and make it fascinating.** Persuasion serves the reader; manipulation works against them.

Never: create false urgency or scarcity, exaggerate results you can't prove, fabricate social proof, use fear beyond what accurately reflects reality, or sell to people the product won't genuinely help. If the draft does any of these, fix it and say why.

Always: make only substantiated claims, respect the reader's intelligence, and ask whether someone who bought based on this copy would feel they were treated honestly.
