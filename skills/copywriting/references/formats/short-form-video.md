# Short-Form Video Scripts
## TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts

---

## The Hook (First 3 Seconds)

Must stop the scroll. Every effective hook creates an unresolved tension the viewer must stay to resolve. The hook must also work visually — most short-form video is watched muted, so bold text overlays and expressive visuals carry the hook before audio does.

**Hook types:**

**Curiosity gap** — Open a loop without closing it.
> "The mistake 90% of founders make before they raise their first dollar..."

**Pain point** — Name a specific frustration the viewer is experiencing right now.
> "If you've sent 50 applications and gotten zero callbacks..."

**Contrarian / pattern interrupt** — Challenge a widely held belief. Creates cognitive dissonance that must be resolved.
> "Everything you've been told about building an audience is backwards."

**Bold promise** — Specific, credible claim of a transformation in a defined timeframe.
> "In the next 60 seconds, you'll know exactly how to open a cold email."

**POV / scene-setting** — Drop the viewer into a recognizable situation they've lived.
> "You're 30, sitting in a meeting you didn't need to attend, staring at a calendar that isn't yours."

---

## The Body

Deliver on the hook's promise immediately. No preamble, no intro. The viewer trusted the hook — pay it off fast.

**Curiosity stacking:** Don't resolve all tension at once. As one question gets answered, introduce another. As one payoff lands, hint at the next. This holds attention through the full video rather than losing viewers once the initial hook is resolved.

**Pattern interrupts:** Build in a change — a new point, a shift in angle, a surprising fact — every 7–10 seconds. The viewer's attention starts to drift from static stimuli; interrupts reset it.

Sentences should be short. Write to be spoken. Energy should match content.

---

## The CTA (Final 5–10 Seconds)

One action only. After delivering genuine value, a CTA lands naturally — don't beg, assume the viewer wants more.

- "I made a full guide on exactly how to do this — link in bio."
- "Follow for more like this."
- "Save this — you'll want to come back to it."

Asking for multiple actions (like, comment, follow, click) dilutes all of them.

---

## Script vs. Outline

Write a structured outline of key talking points, not a word-for-word script. Verbatim scripts produce robotic delivery that undermines the intimacy and personality the format requires. The hook should be precise. The body follows talking points. Don't cram too much into one video — if there's more to say, split it into multiple videos, each with its own hook.

---

## Platform Notes

| Platform | Sweet Spot | Caption Length | Key Signal |
|----------|-----------|----------------|------------|
| TikTok | 15–60s | Up to 4,000 chars (matters for SEO) | Hook must land in first 2s |
| Instagram Reels | 7–30s or 30–60s | 100–300 chars | Algorithm weights 3s retention heavily |
| YouTube Shorts | Up to 60s | Standard YT description | Slightly more patient audience; longer payoffs work |

## What good looks like

---

### Hook quality by type

**Curiosity gap**

Weak:
> "Here are some tips about building an audience."

Strong:
> "What if I told you the reason your ads aren't working has nothing to do with the ads?"

The weak version describes content. The strong version plants a question the viewer can't answer without watching. Notice it challenges an assumption the viewer already has ("my ads are the problem") rather than introducing a topic from scratch.

---

**Pain point**

Weak:
> "Struggling with your business? Here's some advice."

Strong:
> "You're grinding 10 hours a day and your business still isn't profitable? That's not a work ethic issue — it's a model issue."

The strong version names the exact situation with specificity, then immediately reframes it. "Grinding 10 hours a day" is a phrase the target viewer has said or thought. Reframing the cause ("not effort, but model") opens a new loop: what's the model issue?

---

**Contrarian / pattern interrupt**

Weak:
> "Most people think about productivity wrong."

Strong:
> "Stop drinking coffee the second you wake up. If you crash mid-morning, this is why."

The strong version is a direct command, not an opinion. It addresses a specific behavior the viewer likely does, creating immediate personal relevance. The second sentence introduces consequence — the crash — which is the pain that earns the watch.

---

**Bold promise**

Weak:
> "I'm going to show you how to get clients."

Strong:
> "No funnel. No ads. Here's how I landed $20K clients using only DMs."

Specificity does the work: a dollar figure, a named channel (DMs), and two eliminated barriers that the viewer assumed were required. Each specific removes one objection before the viewer forms it.

---

**POV / scene-setting**

Weak:
> "Have you ever felt stuck in your job?"

Strong:
> "You're 30, sitting in a meeting you didn't need to attend, staring at a calendar that isn't yours."

The weak version asks a question the viewer has to stop and answer. The strong version drops them into the scene without asking — the viewer either recognizes it or doesn't. If they recognize it, they're hooked.

---

### Complete script example — curiosity gap + how-to

This script circulates online and demonstrates the full structure in under 60 words:

> "Stop drinking coffee the second you wake up.
>
> If you crash mid-morning, this helps.
>
> Drink water first. Get light on your face for two minutes. Then wait 60 to 90 minutes before coffee.
>
> I did this for a week. My 10 a.m. crash got smaller.
>
> Save this and try it tomorrow. Comment 'COFFEE' if you want my full morning checklist."

**What works:**
The hook is a command directed at a specific behavior, not a topic. The body is three steps with zero preamble — no "hey guys," no "today I'm going to share." Personal proof ("I did this for a week") arrives at exactly one sentence because that's all it needs. The CTA is specific (save + comment), the comment trigger ("COFFEE") creates a data signal, and "tomorrow" turns the advice into an immediate commitment rather than a vague intention.

**What to improve:**
"My 10 a.m. crash got smaller" is a weak proof statement — the result is hedged ("smaller"). A stronger version would be specific: "By day 3 I stopped needing a second cup entirely." The opening command could be even sharper with a brief consequence front-loaded: "If you crash every morning at 10, stop drinking coffee the second you wake up."
