# Social Ad Copy (Meta, LinkedIn, TikTok Ads)

Social ads interrupt. The viewer was not looking for your offer. This changes everything about how the copy works.

---

## Primary Text (Copy Above the Image)

On Meta, primary text truncates at approximately 125 characters on mobile before "See More." The opening line must do the full job within that constraint — spark curiosity, validate a pain point, or promise a specific outcome — before most viewers will expand.

Copy length should match audience temperature. Cold audiences need brief, curiosity-driven copy. Retargeting audiences can handle longer copy that addresses specific objections and concerns.

---

## Headline (Bold Text Below the Image)

Many viewers see the image and headline before reading the primary text. The headline must stand alone as a compelling reason to click — state the core benefit or transformation in 5–8 words.

The CTA should use action-specific language that sets clear expectations about what happens when the reader clicks. Avoid generic labels that say nothing about the value on the other side.

---

## The Image Is Copy

Text overlays, facial expressions, before/after visuals — these communicate before the viewer reads a word. The image and the written copy must reinforce the same idea. When they contradict each other, viewers experience friction that blocks conversion.

---

## Video Ads

Follow the same hook logic as organic short-form video — see `references/formats/short-form-video.md` — with one difference: the viewer knows it's an ad. The hook must overcome that resistance in the first 2–3 seconds. If it doesn't land immediately, the rest of the script doesn't matter.

---

## Targeting and Copy Alignment

Write for a specific audience segment — one type of person in one specific situation. Copy that tries to speak to everyone speaks deeply to no one. The targeting and the copy should be designed together: if you're targeting a specific job title or interest, the copy should reflect the exact language and concerns of that group.

Test different emotional angles — aspiration, fear of loss, curiosity, belonging — to find what resonates with each segment. What works for a cold audience differs from what works for a retargeting audience who already visited the page.

---

## What good looks like

Social ad copy is harder to document verbatim than email — ads rotate, get pulled, and rarely get transcribed in full. The examples below are drawn from documented campaigns. Each is annotated for what the copy is doing and where it could go further.

---

### Opening line — weak vs. strong

**Generic product pitch vs. fear angle**

Weak:
> "Our tree tents are built for outdoor adventures."

Strong:
> "We tested FAKE Tentsile products so you don't have to!"
> — Tentsile

The weak version leads with the product. The strong version leads with a threat the customer didn't know existed — counterfeit products — which reframes the brand as a protector rather than a seller. A viewer who has ever bought outdoor gear from an unfamiliar seller will feel the relevance immediately.

---

**Feature announcement vs. pain-point question**

Weak:
> "T-shirt quilts, made from your old shirts."

Strong:
> "Do you have drawers full of t-shirts you love & cherish, but don't wear? Repurpose them into a meaningful & beautiful quilt."
> — Project Repat

The weak version describes the product. The strong version opens with a question that the target customer will answer "yes" to in their head before they've finished reading — which creates forward momentum into the rest of the copy. The word "cherish" does specific work: it elevates the shirts from clutter to something worth preserving, which is the whole reason someone would pay to have them made into a quilt.

---

### Headline — weak vs. strong

**Generic claim vs. social proof**

Weak:
> "Healthy Dog Food, Delivered"

Strong:
> "MY DOG LOVES THIS STUFF"
> — Nom Nom

The weak headline is category-standard; any dog food subscription brand could run it. The strong version is a customer quote — the claim is social proof, not brand assertion, and the enthusiasm ("LOVES") is unhedged. The all-caps styling mirrors how people write when they're genuinely excited, not when they're writing ad copy.

---

**Vague benefit vs. specific, taboo-adjacent curiosity**

Weak:
> "Smart Tax Strategies for Families"

Strong:
> "Did you know the IRS allows you to hire your children and pay them $6,300 annually, tax-free?"
> — Wealth Factory

The weak headline describes a category. The strong version opens a loop around a specific, counterintuitive claim most people have never heard. The word "IRS" provides authority; the dollar figure is specific enough to be credible; the "did you know" framing positions the reader as someone who should know this and doesn't, creating a knowledge gap they want to close.

---

### Complete ad example — ecommerce, cold audience

**Project Repat**

> Primary text: "Do you have drawers full of t-shirts you love & cherish, but don't wear? Repurpose them into a meaningful & beautiful quilt. Get 15% off today!"
>
> Headline: "Turn Your Old T-Shirts Into a Quilt"
>
> CTA: "Shop Now"

**What works:**
The primary text opens with a pain-point question that qualifies the viewer before they read another word. If they don't have old shirts, they self-select out; if they do, they're immediately engaged. "Love & cherish, but don't wear" names the specific emotional tension — the shirts matter, which is why they've been kept, and it's also why they're cluttering a drawer. The headline delivers the product in plain language for viewers who see only the image and headline. The 15% offer in the primary text gives an immediate reason to act.

**What to improve:**
"Get 15% off today!" undercuts the emotional setup. The copy establishes that these shirts are meaningful, then closes with a discount — which shifts the frame from sentimental value to price. A stronger close would reinforce the transformation: something like "See how it looks before you order." The CTA "Shop Now" is generic; "Start My Quilt" would mirror the journey the ad has set up.
