# Copywriting Frameworks: PAS, AIDA, BAB

These are the three foundational structural frameworks in copywriting. Use them as scaffolding for any format — email, social post, sales page section, video script, or ad.

---

## PAS: Problem → Agitation → Solution

The most reliable structure in copywriting. Works across emails, ads, landing pages, video scripts, and social posts.

**Problem** — Name the specific pain the reader is experiencing. Be precise. Use their language, not marketing language. One concise, accurate description of the problem — the more specifically you name it, the more the reader trusts you to solve it.

**Agitation** — Expand on the consequences of the problem: what has it already cost, what will it cost if nothing changes? The goal is to make the full weight of the problem real. Use the actual words your customers use to describe their frustration. Do not overdramatize or manufacture urgency — exaggerated agitation reads as manipulation and breaks trust. Then: the turn. "It doesn't have to be this way."

**Solution** — Present the product, service, or idea that solves the problem. By this point the reader is primed to want it. The solution must feel like a direct answer to the agitated problem — not a pivot to something adjacent. When PAS works, the solution feels inevitable.

---

## AIDA: Attention → Interest → Desire → Action

The classical advertising framework, developed in 1898 and still sound. Best for interruptive contexts — ads, cold email, content — where you need to earn attention step by step before making any ask.

**Attention** — Stop the reader. This is the headline, opening image, or first line. Must interrupt whatever they were doing. Use a powerful hook: a surprising claim, a named pain, a curiosity gap. You have one moment.

**Interest** — Sustain engagement with facts, data, and relevant information that speaks directly to their situation. This is where you earn the right to their continued attention. Engage their mind, not just their emotions — specifics, statistics, and insight do the work here.

**Desire** — Move the reader from "this is interesting" to "I want this." Show them what life looks like after — vivid, specific, sensory. Real evidence does the most work here: before/after results, testimonials, proof of transformation. Find the deep emotional benefit and name it.

**Action** — Tell them exactly what to do next. One clear, specific step. Make it easy and obvious. Frame it as a call to value, not a call to click.

---

## BAB: Before → After → Bridge

Best for copy built around transformation: testimonials, case studies, aspirational content, cold email. Makes the reader the hero of the story by showing them their current reality and then their possible future before introducing anything you're selling.

**Before** — Describe the reader's current reality accurately and empathetically. Don't sugarcoat it. Use their exact language for their frustration. The reader should recognize themselves immediately.

**After** — Paint a vivid, specific picture of the transformed reality. Concrete details matter here — the more specific the after, the more the reader can see and want it. Show the emotional payoff: the relief, the pride, the freedom.

**Bridge** — Your product, service, or idea is the path between the two states. This is where you introduce what you're offering — and why specifically it creates the transformation. Social proof belongs here: testimonials and results that make the bridge credible, not just claimed.

**Important constraint:** BAB works for positive transformations — gaining something desirable. It doesn't work well for avoiding negative outcomes (insurance, security products, risk mitigation). For those, PAS is the better frame.

---

## Choosing Between Them

- **PAS** — when the reader has a problem they're aware of and you want to validate it before solving it
- **AIDA** — when you need to earn attention from scratch, step by step, in an interruptive context
- **BAB** — when the transformation is the most compelling thing, and the reader is seeking a positive gain

All three can be combined and nested. A sales page might open with AIDA to earn attention, use BAB for testimonials, and use PAS to handle each objection.
