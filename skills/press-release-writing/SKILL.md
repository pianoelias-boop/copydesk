# Press Release Writing Skill

## Before You Write: The Newsworthiness Test

A press release is a news document, not a marketing document. Before drafting, ask:

1. **Is this actually news?** If it only matters internally, it's not news. Newsworthy announcements typically involve: product launches that solve a real problem, major milestones, leadership changes, partnerships, awards, research findings, or events.
2. **Who outside the company cares?** If the answer is unclear, help the user find a stronger angle before writing.
3. **What's the broader context?** The best press releases connect company news to industry trends, market shifts, or audience concerns. "XYZ launches new software" is weak. "XYZ launches software addressing a gap in SMB inventory management" is news.

If the announcement doesn't pass the test, flag it diplomatically and help find a better angle first.

## How a Press Release Is Structured

Press releases follow the **inverted pyramid**: lead with the most important information, then layer in supporting detail, context, and background. Journalists read top-down and cut from the bottom up — so nothing critical should live in the lower half.

The general flow:

1. **Headline** — the news in one clear line
2. **Subheadline** (optional) — adds a key stat or supporting context
3. **Dateline** — city, state, date
4. **Lead paragraph** — who, what, when, where, why
5. **Body** — supporting detail, data, context, quotes
6. **Boilerplate** — standard company description
7. **Contact information**

---

## Sample Template

This is a scaffold, not a script. Adapt structure, length, and tone to the announcement.

```
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE  (or EMBARGOED UNTIL: [Date])

[Headline]
[Subheadline — optional]

CITY, State, Month Day, Year —

[Lead paragraph: the core news in 2-3 sentences. Who did what, and why does it matter?]

[Body: supporting context, data, and detail. Build out the story — industry significance,
how it works, who it affects. Keep paragraphs tight: 3-4 sentences max.]

[Quote from a company spokesperson or executive — adds a human voice and perspective.]

[Additional detail or secondary quote if needed — a customer, partner, or analyst.]

[Boilerplate: 2-3 sentences on who the company is and what it does.]

###

Media Contact:
Name
Title
Email
Phone (include mobile)
```

## Element-by-Element Writing Guide

### Headline
The headline is often the only thing a journalist reads. Make it count.

- Keep it under 90 characters; aim for 60 if possible
- Capitalize only the first word and proper nouns (AP style)
- Lead with the news, not the company name
- Be specific — vague headlines get ignored
- ❌ "Acme Corp Proud to Announce New Solution for Enterprise Clients"
- ✅ "Acme Corp Launches AI Scheduling Tool That Cuts Booking Time by 40%"

### Subheadline (Optional)
Adds a key stat, benefit, or context that didn't fit in the headline. One sentence only.

### Dateline
Format: `CITY, State Abbreviation, Month Day, Year —`
Example: `NEW YORK, March 3, 2026 —`
Well-known cities (New York, Chicago, Los Angeles) don't need the state.

### Lead Paragraph
- Answer the 5 W's: who, what, when, where, why — in 2-3 sentences
- Write it like a news story opener, not an ad
- ❌ "Today, we're thrilled to announce an incredible milestone..."
- ✅ "ActiveCampaign today launched [Product], an AI-powered tool that [does X], giving SMBs the ability to [achieve outcome] without [pain point]."

### Body
- Inverted pyramid throughout: most important → least important
- 3-4 sentences per paragraph
- Include: market context, statistics, product/announcement detail, timeline
- When citing surveys or research, always include the sample size
- Bullet points are fine for key features or stats if they aid scannability — use sparingly
- Don't bury the lead. If something is important, it belongs near the top.

### Quotes
- Include at least one quote — from a company exec, spokesperson, or a customer/partner
- Quotes should add human perspective or context, not restate what the paragraph already says
- Attribute with full name and title on first reference
- AP format: `"Quote text," said FirstName LastName, Title, Company.`
- ❌ "We are incredibly excited to share this groundbreaking announcement," said CEO Jane Smith.
- ✅ "SMBs have been duct-taping scheduling tools together for years. This removes that entirely," said Jane Smith, CEO of Acme Corp.

### Boilerplate
- 2-3 sentences max — standardize it and reuse across all releases
- Covers: what the company does, who it serves, a key differentiator
- End with: "For more information, visit [URL]."

### Contact Block
- Always include a **mobile number** — journalists work on tight deadlines and may call outside business hours
- Include: name, title, email, and phone
- Do not use a generic inbox like info@company.com

## AP Style Quick Reference

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| Numbers | Spell out one through nine; numerals for 10 and above |
| Ages & percentages | Always use numerals (5%, 3-year-old) |
| Dates with a specific day | Abbreviate: Jan., Feb., Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., Dec. |
| Dates standalone | Spell out: "August 2026" |
| Job titles before a name | Capitalize: "CEO Jane Doe" |
| Job titles after a name | Lowercase: "Jane Doe, chief executive officer" |
| Acronyms | Spell out on first use with abbreviation in parentheses: "artificial intelligence (AI)" |
| Oxford comma | Do NOT use: "red, white and blue" |
| Spacing after period | One space |
| Quotation marks | Double quotes; single quotes within quotes |

## Words & Phrases to Avoid

Journalists tune out — or delete — press releases containing:

**Buzzwords:** revolutionary, groundbreaking, game-changing, paradigm shift, synergistic, disruptive, innovative, best-in-class, world-class, cutting-edge, next-generation, seamless, robust

**Vague descriptors:** leading, premier, top, trusted, comprehensive

**Sales-speak:** excited to announce, proud to share, thrilled to introduce, honored to

**Filler:** "in today's rapidly evolving landscape," "at the intersection of X and Y," "leveraging our expertise"

Replace all of the above with specific facts, concrete outcomes, and plain language.

## Press Release Types: Key Angles to Hit

| Type | What to Emphasize |
|------|------------------|
| Product launch | Problem it solves, key features (pick 2-3), availability |
| Funding announcement | Round size, lead investor, use of funds, company traction |
| Partnership | What each party brings, who benefits, why now |
| Executive hire/change | Brief bio, why this person, what it signals about direction |
| Award/recognition | The award, the granting body, what criteria were met |
| Research/report | Methodology, sample size, 2-3 headline findings |
| Event | Date, location, who should attend, key speakers |
| Rebrand | What changed, why, what stays the same |
| Acquisition/merger | Strategic rationale, what it means for customers |

## Pre-Send Writing Checklist

- [ ] Headline is under 90 characters and free of buzzwords
- [ ] Lede answers who, what, when, where, why
- [ ] No paragraph is longer than 4 sentences
- [ ] Quotes add insight — not just enthusiasm
- [ ] All stats include a source and sample size
- [ ] No unexplained jargon or acronyms
- [ ] Boilerplate is present and accurate
- [ ] Mobile number is in the contact block
- [ ] Total length is under 600 words
- [ ] Ends with ### followed by contact info
- [ ] AP style followed throughout

---

## Output Format

When writing a press release, deliver:
1. The **complete press release** — properly formatted, ready to send
2. A suggested **email subject line** for journalist outreach — direct, under 60 characters, no hype words

Ask for any missing details before drafting (who should be quoted, release date, any embargo, key stats or proof points to include).
