# Long-Form Video Script Skill

Framework for planning, scripting, and critiquing long-form YouTube videos for ActiveCampaign's brand channel. Target length: **5–20 minutes**. May also be embedded on the ActiveCampaign website.

---

## How to Use This Skill

**Determine which mode applies and proceed accordingly:**

- **Mode A — Outline**: User has a topic or brief. Produce a structured outline with segment-by-segment beats. **This is the default.** Do not write a full script unless explicitly asked.
- **Mode B — Full script**: User explicitly asks for a written script, OR has approved an outline and wants to move to draft. Write word-for-word where it matters (hook, transitions, payoffs), bullet-point where the speaker will riff.
- **Mode C — Feedback**: User provides an existing outline or script. Diagnose against the framework. Provide prioritized notes. Offer to revise specific sections on request.

If the user's request is ambiguous, ask: *"Are you starting from scratch, or do you have a draft or outline to work from?"*

## Video Format Types

Apply format-specific guidance based on the video type. Ask the user if unclear.

### 1. Product / Feature Walkthrough
The video demonstrates ActiveCampaign functionality in action. Common for Active Intelligence, automations, or new feature releases.

**Key principles:**
- Open with the outcome, not the tool. Show what it enables before showing how it works.
- The demo is not the video — the story is the video. The demo is evidence.
- Every feature shown should be anchored to a specific use case (e.g., "imagine you're a gym owner with 3,000 contacts..."). Abstract demos lose viewers.
- Keep the "wow" moment early — within the first 2 minutes. Don't save the most impressive thing for the end.

### 2. Thought Leadership / Educational
The video makes a strong argument or teaches a concept. Audience is marketers who care about email, automation, AI in marketing.

**Key principles:**
- The idea must be counterintuitive, underserved, or contrarian. Don't make the same video everyone else is making.
- Lead with a sharp, specific claim — not a vague topic. "Why your open rate is lying to you" beats "Email marketing tips."
- Use a tension loop structure: pose a problem/question → develop it → resolve it. The viewer must feel they need the resolution.
- Proof points and examples anchor the argument. Use the AC proof points skill to pull verified stats.

### 3. Customer Story / Use Case
The video tells the story of how a real customer or marketer used ActiveCampaign or AI to get results. Closest to a mini-documentary.

**Key principles:**
- Structure as a story arc, not a testimonial. Problem → attempt → breakthrough → result.
- The protagonist is the customer or marketer — not ActiveCampaign.
- Specificity is everything. "She was sending 2 emails a week to 4,000 contacts with a 12% open rate" beats "she had a small list."
- The "before" state needs to be felt, not just stated. Spend time on the pain before introducing the solution.

## The Structure Framework

### Pre-Writing: Packaging First
*(Ed Lawrence, Paddy Galloway)*

**Do not write a single line of script until the packaging is locked.**

The packaging = title + thumbnail concept. If you can't articulate a title that would make the target viewer click, the concept isn't ready.

Ask:
- What is the one thing this video promises the viewer?
- Who exactly is the viewer who would click this? (e.g., "a B2B marketer frustrated with low email open rates," not just "marketers")
- Would this title make *that specific person* stop scrolling?

**Concept test:** Complete this in 10 words or fewer:
> *"This video is worth watching because ___."*

If you can't, reframe before outlining.

---

### The Minute-by-Minute Structure
*(MrBeast production guide, adapted for brand content)*

Long-form YouTube videos have predictable drop-off patterns. Structure the video around these windows:

| Window | Function | Drop-off risk |
|---|---|---|
| **0:00–1:00** | Hook and promise. Highest drop-off. Must prove the video will deliver what the title/thumbnail promised. | **Highest** |
| **1:00–3:00** | Establish the stakes. Deepen the viewer's investment in the outcome. | High |
| **3:00–6:00** | The heart of the content. Most exciting/dense material. Quick scene changes, simple ideas clearly explained. "Make them fall in love with the story." | Medium |
| **6:00–end** | Resolution, payoff, and close. Re-engagement moments to prevent late drop-off. | Medium-low |

**Re-engagement rule:** In any video over 5 minutes, include a deliberate re-engagement moment at approximately the 3-minute mark and every 3–4 minutes thereafter. A re-engagement can be: a new reveal, a surprising statistic, a scene change, a question posed to the viewer, or a tease of what's coming.

**End strong:** The last 60 seconds matter more than most creators realize. The viewer's final emotional state is how they'll remember (and share) the video. Don't trail off into housekeeping. End on the highest-intensity moment or insight, then close cleanly.

---

### Segment Structure: Setup → Tension → Payoff
*(George Blackman)*

Every segment within the video — not just the video as a whole — should follow this arc:

- **Setup**: Why does this segment matter? Orient the viewer before delivering information.
- **Tension**: Build toward the reveal. Add information incrementally. Create a sense that something important is coming.
- **Payoff**: Deliver the thing the viewer has been waiting for. Don't bury it.

**The transition test:** The sentences between segments are the highest drop-off risk. Write these in full, even if the rest of the segment is bulleted. The viewer decides whether to keep watching at every transition.

Transition formula: *End segment → tease what's next → begin next segment.*
> "So we've seen how [X] works. But here's where it gets interesting — because the same approach that [X], when combined with [Y], produces something most marketers have never seen before."

---

### Hook Construction: Target → Transformation → Stakes
*(Ed Lawrence / Film Booth)*

The hook is the first 30–60 seconds of a long-form video (longer than short-form, but not unlimited). It has one job: make the viewer believe this video is worth the next 10 minutes of their life.

**Three elements every hook must address:**

1. **Target** — Signal to the right viewer that this video is for them. Name their situation, role, or frustration explicitly.
2. **Transformation** — What will they know, be able to do, or understand by the end that they don't now?
3. **Stakes** — Why does this matter? What's the cost of not knowing this?

**Hook archetypes for brand content:**

| Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Bold claim | "[Surprising statement about their situation]" | "Most email marketers are optimizing for the wrong metric — and it's quietly killing their revenue." |
| Story drop | Open mid-story, establish intrigue, then zoom out | "Last year, a gym owner with a list of 3,000 contacts sent one automation. It generated $47,000 in 30 days. Here's exactly what she did." |
| Challenge assumption | "[Common belief] is wrong. Here's what's actually true." | "Everyone says personalization is the future of email. But the data tells a different story." |
| Problem frame | Describe a specific, painful situation in detail | "You've been building automations for months. Your open rates look okay. But your reply rates are near zero and you can't figure out why." |
| Proof-first | Lead with a remarkable result, then promise to explain how | "This campaign had a 47% open rate. The subject line was four words. I'm going to show you the exact reasoning behind every decision." |

**Hook rules:**
- Do not open with brand introduction. Do not say "Hi, I'm [name] from ActiveCampaign." Earn the viewer first.
- Never tease something you won't deliver fully within the video.
- The last line of the hook should be a clear statement of what the viewer will get. Make it explicit.

### The "Wow Factor"
*(MrBeast production guide)*

Every long-form video should have at least one moment that makes the viewer think: *only ActiveCampaign / only this team could have made this.*

For a brand video, the wow factor might be:
- A live build that produces something remarkable in real time (product demo)
- A result so specific and impressive that it's hard to believe (customer story)
- An insight so counterintuitive it reframes something the viewer thought they understood (thought leadership)
- A visual or production moment that signals real investment and confidence

Ask: *What is the single most impressive thing we can show or say in this video? Is it early enough?*

The wow factor should appear within the first 2 minutes, or be clearly teased in the hook.

### Mode B — Full Script

Write each segment with:
- **Full word-for-word text** for: hook, all segment transitions, payoff moments, and close
- **Bullet-point beats** for: mid-segment explanations where the presenter will naturally riff
- **[VISUAL]** notes for on-screen graphics, demo moments, or B-roll
- **[ON SCREEN: "text"]** for key phrases to display as on-screen text

Flag any segment where the script feels weak against the Setup → Tension → Payoff structure.

## ActiveCampaign-Specific Guidance

**Strong concept territory for long-form:**
- "How we built a [remarkable result] using AI + automation" (customer story)
- Counterintuitive takes on email, automation, or AI marketing metrics
- Live demos that produce something impressive in real time (Active Intelligence builds)
- Deep dives into specific use cases: franchise marketing, ecommerce re-engagement, B2B lead nurturing
- Competitive angles: "Why marketers are switching from Mailchimp" (with specifics, not generic claims)

**Proof points:** Always pull from the AC proof points skill. Do not invent statistics or use approximate figures.

**Voice:** Direct, confident, human. The presenter is a knowledgeable peer, not a salesperson. Avoid: corporate language, feature-first framing, hype without evidence.

**ActiveCampaign should not be the hero of the story.** The marketer is. ActiveCampaign is the tool that made it possible.

## Common Mistakes to Flag

- **No packaging before scripting**: Script was written before the title/thumbnail concept was clear. Usually produces unfocused videos.
- **Feature-first demo**: The product walkthrough starts with the UI before establishing why any viewer should care.
- **Missing re-engagement**: Video over 5 minutes with no deliberate re-hook after the opening. Drop-off is guaranteed.
- **Weak transitions**: Segments end flatly with no bridge to the next. Every transition is a click-away opportunity.
- **Buried wow factor**: The most impressive moment comes at the 9-minute mark of a 10-minute video. Move it earlier or tease it in the hook.
- **Soft ending**: Video trails off into "so yeah, that's basically it" energy. The last 60 seconds should have the highest intensity.
- **Generic claims**: "ActiveCampaign saves you time" without specifics. Always anchor to a real number, story, or example.
- **Hook disease**: Opening with "Today I'm going to be talking about..." or any version of announcing the topic rather than doing it.
