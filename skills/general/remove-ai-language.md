# Remove AI Language

> **PLACEHOLDER SKILL** — replace this file's contents with your own AI-language-removal skill. The pipeline sends this entire file to the editor model, so write it as direct editorial rules.

Strip the verbal tics that make writing read as AI-generated. The goal is prose that sounds like a person with a point of view.

## Words and phrases to remove or replace

- **Inflated verbs**: delve, unlock, unleash, harness, leverage (as a verb), elevate, empower, supercharge, revolutionize, transform (when nothing is being transformed), streamline, seamlessly
- **Inflated adjectives**: cutting-edge, game-changing, robust, comprehensive, innovative, dynamic, holistic, pivotal, crucial (when merely relevant), vibrant, bustling
- **Empty framing**: "In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-evolving landscape of X", "Now more than ever", "It's no secret that", "Look no further", "At the end of the day"
- **Hedge stacking**: "can potentially help to", "may be able to" — commit to a claim or cut it
- **Summary crutches**: "In conclusion", "Overall", "Ultimately" as paragraph openers
- **The em-dash pile-up**: more than one em-dash per paragraph reads as generated; restructure
- **Triplet addiction**: AI defaults to lists of three ("faster, smarter, better"). Break the pattern unless the rhythm genuinely serves the line.

## Structural tells

- Every paragraph the same length. Vary paragraph length deliberately.
- Symmetrical "Not only X, but also Y" constructions. Use sparingly or not at all.
- Section headers that restate the obvious ("Why This Matters").
- Closing paragraphs that summarize what was just said. End on the strongest point instead.

## What to do instead

- Replace abstractions with specifics: numbers, names, concrete nouns.
- Prefer short Anglo-Saxon verbs over Latinate ones: "use" not "utilize", "help" not "facilitate".
- One idea per sentence. If a sentence has two "and"s and a "which", split it.
- Keep the author's claims exactly as strong as they wrote them — no stronger, no weaker.
