# Copywriting Fundamentals

Editorial rules that apply to all marketing copy.

## Clarity

- Lead with the reader's problem or desired outcome, not the company or product.
- One sentence, one idea. Cut sentences that exist only to set up the next sentence.
- Replace jargon with the words the customer would actually use to describe their problem.
- If a sentence works without a word, the word goes. Target ruthless economy: "in order to" → "to", "is able to" → "can", "a number of" → "several" or the actual number.

## Benefit over feature

- Every feature mention should answer "so what?" within the same breath. Feature → benefit → outcome.
- Specific beats general: "reply to tickets 40% faster" beats "improve efficiency".
- Claims need evidence nearby: a number, a customer name, a concrete mechanism.

## Voice and rhythm

- Active voice unless the actor is genuinely unknown or unimportant.
- Second person ("you") for the reader; first person plural ("we") sparingly.
- Read sentences aloud in your head — anything you'd stumble over gets rewritten.
- Vary sentence length. Short sentences carry punch. Use them where it counts.

## Calls to action

- One primary CTA per piece. Secondary CTAs must not compete visually or verbally.
- CTA verbs describe what the reader gets, not what they do: "Get the report" beats "Submit".
- Remove friction language near CTAs ("just", "simply" — if it were simple you wouldn't need to say so).
